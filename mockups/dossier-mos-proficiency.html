B2cHeader badge only — pips + tier name, no % visible. Tap to expand reveals % and full breakdown. Most "rank insignia" feel. Tightest version.

B2a — Slim chip:
  • Pro: smallest delta from B2. Keeps the chip as a self-contained "stat row" pattern.
  • Pro: still shows % explicitly, which is useful info.
  • Con: doesn't fix the upper-right dead space — the chip is below the header.
  • Width drop: ~25%. Still wider than B1.
B2b — Header inline (full info):
  • Pro: fills the dead upper-right corner exactly. Two stones, one bird.
  • Pro: saves a full row of vertical space (~32px) which matters on mobile landscape.
  • Pro: ties MOS and proficiency visually as one unit — they belong together conceptually.
  • Con: harder to scan when comparing multiple soldiers — proficiency lives in the header rather than a predictable chip row.
  • Con: when MOS dropdown is long ("HEAVY GUNNER"), the inline pill might wrap on narrow screens. Need testing.
B2c — Header badge only:
  • Pro: most "rank insignia" feel. VETERAN reads as a *title* you've earned, not a metric.
  • Pro: tightest layout. Fits comfortably even on small screens.
  • Con: % is hidden until you tap. For min-maxers who want to see "am I at 78 or 79?" this is a friction.
  • Con: tier transitions become more important (going from 79 → 80 = REGULAR → VETERAN feels big; staying within a tier feels invisible).
My take:
  • B2b is the cleanest solve for both complaints (size + dead space). You get the full tier flavor without losing the number, and you reclaim a row of vertical space.
  • If you want minimum-density / max-flavor, B2c is the tightest. The hidden % is fine if we expose it in the tap-to-expand state.
  • B2a only addresses size, not space — I'd skip it unless you want the chip pattern preserved for consistency reasons.
